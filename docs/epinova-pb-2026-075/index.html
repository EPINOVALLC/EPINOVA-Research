The Reconfiguration of War Power

Cross-Theater Coupling, Strategic Capacity, and Asymmetric War Termination

  • Wu, Shaoyuan
    Global AI Governance and Policy Research Center, EPINOVA LLC
    https://orcid.org/0009-0008-0660-8232

Description

This policy brief examines how the Russia–Ukraine war and the U.S.–Israel–Iran conflict interact through shared military resources, industrial capacity, technological knowledge, energy markets, and institutional commitments. It develops Cross-Theater Conflict Coupling (CTCC) as an evidence-gated framework distinguishing shared interfaces, activation, receipt, recipient-side conversion, functional consequences, and feedback. The brief compares PAC-3 allocation pressure, historical Shahed–Geran industrial adaptation, Ukrainian counter-drone expertise in Gulf states, sanctions-conditioned Russian diesel trade, and strategic petroleum reserve accounting. It then examines asymmetric war termination through conditional scenarios involving localized humanitarian pauses, nationwide armistices, prolonged confrontation, and hypothetical Russian occupation of Ukraine. It argues that lower battlefield consumption does not automatically release transferable strategic capacity: the net effects depend on authorization, ownership, pre-existing obligations, replenishment, transfer time, recipient-side usability, and identifiable feedback.

Abstract

The strategic problem is not whether the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East touch the same supply chains, technologies, policies, and markets. They do. The question for decision-makers is whether a change originating in one conflict actually alters what forces and institutions in another conflict can do, and when. CTCC (Cross-Theater Conflict Coupling) provides a structured test, separating shared exposure from activated transmission, delivery, conversion, functional consequence, and feedback. The evidence recorded through 23 October 2026 points to distinct mechanisms rather than a single pattern of reciprocal support. Russian adaptation of Iranian-origin Shahed designs is historically documented industrial learning, but it does not prove new 2026 Iranian-to-Russian or Russian-to-Iranian deliveries. Ukrainian specialists have operated in Gulf states, yet their net effect on interceptions has not been independently measured. The Iran war has raised identifiable pressure within the PAC-3 and allied procurement system, but the Ukraine-specific number of missiles displaced cannot be established. U.S. licensing and a named Russian diesel tanker show progression in a commercial chain without confirming U.S. import, incremental refinery output, or additional Russian war finance.
